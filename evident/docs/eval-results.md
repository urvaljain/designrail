All data is synthetic and hand-labelled (see `evals/data.ts`). Sample sizes are small and are stated next to every figure. No live model and no real user was involved.

**In-sample caveat.** Earlier runs of this harness exposed defects (two injection false positives, a skill alias matching inside "Node.js", over-generic aliases, a must-have cue read as optional) that were then fixed. The figures below are measured after those fixes on the same data, so they overstate how the rules would do on unseen text. `docs/08-evaluation-report.md` lists what changed and the out-of-sample checks that exist.

## 3. Prompt injection through job descriptions

- Detection rate: **100%** (14 of 14 attack sentences flagged)
- False-positive rate on benign job-description sentences: **0%** (0 of 14)
- Detection is a tripwire, not the defence. The defence is structural: job text is never sent to a model (integration test `never sends injected job text to a model`), requirements are extracted by rules from text with flagged sentences removed, and model output is verified against the resume.
